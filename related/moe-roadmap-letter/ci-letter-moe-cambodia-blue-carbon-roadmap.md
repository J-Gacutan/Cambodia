## Internal note, not for sending

**What changed since the first draft.** The first draft said the record held nothing on a roadmap, and that CI Cambodia's programme covered only land and freshwater. Both statements were wrong. CI Cambodia's own June 2026 report, prepared with CI's Blue Climate programme and IBCI, fills the gap:

- **The roadmap.** "The Department of Biodiversity of MoE plans to develop a Blue Carbon Roadmap" (p. 20, SWOT table, opportunities). UNDP is "assisting the Department of Biodiversity of MoE in integrating the development of a national blue carbon roadmap into the 2026 BioFin workplan" (p. 12). BioFin is UNDP's Biodiversity Finance Initiative.
- **CI's role.** The report's findings "are formulated as policy recommendations to help inform the development of a national blue carbon roadmap in Cambodia" (p. 4). So CI's support is already under way. The letter offers the report's findings to the roadmap and proposes follow-on support. It does not propose a new relationship.
- **CI's track record in Cambodia on blue carbon.** CI organised Cambodia's first national blue carbon workshop, held in Kampot in April 2024 (p. 12). IBCI is partnering with Fauna & Flora on a mangrove and seagrass feasibility study in early 2026 (p. 12). The first draft's reference to the IBCI regional workshop is dropped: the Kampot workshop and the report are stronger common ground.

**Addressee.** The letter goes to the Director of the Department of Biodiversity, which leads the roadmap. It is copied to the General Directorate of Policy and Strategy (GDPS) and its Department of Climate Change (DCC). DCC coordinates the NDC and the GHG inventory, and the report notes that "without DCC endorsement, provisions related to blue carbon may not be integrated into the climate related policies documents" (p. 12). CI Cambodia should confirm the addressee's name and title, and whether the letter should go through the Minister's cabinet. `[to confirm]`

**Sender.** The letter could come from CI Cambodia's Country Director, Sony Oum, with IBCI co-signing, or from IBCI with CI Cambodia co-signing. CI Cambodia carries the country relationship and authored the report, so a CI Cambodia lead is the default. `[to confirm]`

**UNDP.** UNDP is already supporting the roadmap through BioFin. The letter should present CI's offer as complementary to UNDP's support, not in competition with it. CI Cambodia should say whether UNDP has been told about this letter, or should be copied. `[to confirm]`

**Contract basis.** This is not one of D5 to D10. Under Amendment 002 it can only be done as a D11 task, and a D11 task has to be confirmed by CI by email, with a day allocation, before work starts. Confirm with Fiona Cromarty. `[to confirm]`

**D5 sequencing.** Under the D5 approach, no government is approached on the D5 proposal topics until Singapore's Ministry of Sustainability and Environment (MSE) has confirmed in writing whether it will act as Proponent. The letter stays on national roadmap support and says nothing about ASEAN cooperation projects, MSE or any regional proposal. Fiona Cromarty to confirm. `[to confirm]`

**Support to the development of Cambodia's national Blue Carbon Roadmap**

Conservation International Cambodia and the International Blue Carbon Institute have the honour to write to the Department of Biodiversity regarding the development of Cambodia's national Blue Carbon Roadmap.

We warmly welcome the Ministry's leadership in this work. Cambodia's mangroves and seagrass meadows, some 58,852 hectares and 13,890 hectares along 435 kilometres of coastline, protect coastal communities, support fisheries and store significant amounts of carbon. The Royal Government has already made strong commitments to them. NDC 3.0 sets a target to restore 4,080 hectares of mangroves by 2035, includes blue carbon habitats in its forest management measures, and commits the Ministry to integrated marine spatial planning and to expanding coastal and marine protected areas. The National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan 2026-2030 aims to bring at least 10 per cent of marine and coastal areas under protection by 2030. The Cambodia Climate Change Strategic Plan 2024-2033 calls for advancing blue carbon development through a specific roadmap, and Cambodia joined the Blue NDC Challenge at COP30.

Conservation International has been glad to support this work since the first national workshop on coastal blue carbon conservation and restoration, held in Kampot in April 2024. In June 2026 we completed a study, *Conserving and restoring Cambodia's coastal blue carbon habitats: Policy and finance analysis and stakeholder mapping*, with contributions from government counterparts, technical experts and stakeholders. Its recommendations were prepared to inform the Roadmap, and we would be pleased to present them to the Department and its partners.

Conservation International and the International Blue Carbon Institute would be glad to support the Roadmap in whichever areas the Ministry considers most useful, in coordination with UNDP's support through BioFin. Drawing on the study, these could include:

- **Institutional pathway:** options for bringing seagrass within the mandates of the REDD+ Taskforce and its Secretariat, and a phased pathway for blue carbon within Cambodia's emerging nesting and carbon crediting framework;
- **National policy alignment:** technical input so that the revision of the National REDD+ Strategy, due by the end of 2026, and future NDC updates can consider seagrass alongside mangroves, in line with IPCC guidance for coastal wetlands, and so that coastal wetlands can in time be reported in the national greenhouse gas inventory;
- **Data and mapping:** support for an integrated national blue carbon map of mangroves and seagrass, drawing on data held by the Ministry, the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and other institutions;
- **Carbon stock assessment and capacity:** training in standard field and laboratory methods, building on the International Blue Carbon Institute's mangrove and seagrass feasibility work with Fauna & Flora; and
- **Regional and international exchange:** sharing experience from other ASEAN Member States, and support for Cambodia's engagement on coastal ecosystems in the lead-up to UNFCCC COP31, to be held in Antalya from 9 to 20 November 2026.

These areas are offered for discussion, and the Ministry's own priorities for the Roadmap will guide any support. We would welcome the opportunity to meet the Department, in person or online, to present the study and discuss next steps. `[Sey Peou, Policy and Partnership Manager, Conservation International Cambodia, psey@conservation.org]` will be glad to assist with arrangements.

Please accept, `[title and name]`, the assurances of our highest consideration.
