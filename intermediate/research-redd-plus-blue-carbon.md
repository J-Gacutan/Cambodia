Desk research, web only, run 20 November 2026. No one was contacted. Intermediate file for T3 (Cambodia blue carbon seed grant and REDD+ scoping).

## C. International rules

| # | Point | V/U | Source (title, URL, page) | Implication for Cambodia |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Decision 1/CP.16 para 70** encourages developing countries "to contribute to mitigation actions **in the forest sector**" through five activities: reducing emissions from deforestation; reducing emissions from forest degradation; conservation of forest carbon stocks; sustainable management of forests; enhancement of forest carbon stocks. The text does not mention wetlands, mangroves or seagrass. Appendix I safeguard 2(e) says actions must not be used for the conversion of natural forests. | V | FCCC/CP/2010/7/Add.1, https://unfccc.int/resource/docs/2010/cop16/eng/07a01.pdf (archive.org copy), PDF p.12 para 70; PDF p.26-27 Appendix I. | REDD+ activities attach to forest. Seagrass and tidal marsh are not forest under Cambodia's definition (A1), so they cannot be REDD+ activities without redefining forest, which would also change reporting to FAO and the inventory. |
| C2 | **Forest definition is national.** Decision 12/CP.17 annex asks Parties to state the forest definition used and any difference from the one used in the inventory or other reporting. The UNFCCC assessors checked Cambodia's definition against its FAO reporting. | V (assessor text); U (decision text) | TAR FCCC/TAR/2017/KHM PDF p.7 para 29; Second FRL PDF p.9 (quotes the decision list). Decision 12/CP.17 itself not opened. | Mangroves are in by the national definition, seagrass out. Keep the same definition across REDD+, the inventory and any Article 6 or Verra project. |
| C3 | **IPCC 2013 Wetlands Supplement, Chapter 4 (Coastal Wetlands).** Covers mangroves, tidal marshes and seagrass meadows. "It is good practice to report mangroves in the appropriate national land-use category according to the national forest definition." Mangroves meeting the forest definition stay Forest Land; mangroves not meeting all thresholds but with trees are Wetlands; seagrass meadows and tidal marshes are Wetlands. | V | IPCC, 2013 Wetlands Supplement Ch.4, https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/wetlands/pdf/Wetlands_separate_files/WS_Chp4_Coastal_Wetlands.pdf, printed p.4.6 (Table 4.1 footnote 2) and p.4.8. | The Wetlands Supplement is the method basis for seagrass and marsh, and for any mangrove soil term. It also confirms the land-category split that decides which accounting route a given hectare takes. |
| C4 | **Wetlands Supplement Tier 1 for mangrove soil:** for forest management practices in mangroves the Tier 1 default for soil CO2 is zero (EF=0), modifiable at higher tiers. For extraction (excavation, aquaculture ponds, salt ponds) Tier 1 assumes biomass, dead organic matter and soil are all removed and emitted as CO2 in the year of extraction. Default mangrove AGB, tropical wet: 192 t d.m./ha (95% CI 187-204). | V | Same document, printed p.4.18-4.19 (soil and extraction), p.4.14 (Table 4.3). | The two main mangrove loss pathways in Cambodia (clearance, aquaculture conversion) are very different in soil terms: Tier 1 gives zero for forest clearance and everything for pond excavation. A national mangrove soil factor (Tier 2) is where the carbon is. Cambodia's own FRL mangrove AGB (95 t/ha, A6) is about half the IPCC default. |
| C5 | **ART TREES 3.0 (approved June 2026; current version).** Participants are national governments or subnational governments one level down. National-scale accounting means at least 90% of areas qualifying as forest under the national forest definition; excluded areas must be "isolated, patchy and historically not subject to deforestation rates greater than half of the national rate". Forest definition "must be consistent with the most recent definition(s) used by the national government in REDD+ reporting under the UNFCCC". Pools: primary AGB and **soil organic matter (peat soils)**; secondary BGB, deadwood, litter and **soil organic matter (mineral soils)**. Secondary pools may be left out if conservative, or each under 3% of total emissions and all excluded pools together under 10%. Primary pools must use IPCC Tier 2/3. The standard text does not use the words mangrove, seagrass, tidal or blue carbon. | V | TREES 3.0, https://www.artredd.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/TREES-3.0-Final-Version-English.pdf, PDF p.24, p.31, p.47, p.52. Landing page https://www.artredd.org/trees-3-0/ lists TREES 2.0 as superseded. TREES 2.0 (Aug 2021) pool list at PDF p.32 is the same in substance. | Under a national ART programme Cambodia's mangroves would be in the accounting area automatically (about 0.7% of forest area, so excluding them would meet the 90% test only if they are "isolated, patchy and not subject to high deforestation"). Mangrove soil is mineral or organic by site: if it meets the organic soil definition it is a primary pool needing Tier 2/3; if mineral it is secondary and can be left out under the 3%/10% rule. ## Constraints that shape the options

1. **Definition gate.** Mangrove that meets 5 m / 0.5 ha / 10% is already forest and already in the FRL (A1, A2). Seagrass and tidal marsh are not forest and cannot be REDD+ activities (C1, C3, C10). Changing this means redefining forest, which affects the inventory and FAO reporting. Cheaper: keep mangrove inside REDD+/forest accounting and treat seagrass and marsh as a separate wetland route.
2. **The reference level is deforestation-only and has no mangrove stratum.** The 2021 FRL covers deforestation alone, pools AGB+BGB, with mangrove lumped into "Other forest" (A5, A6). Mangrove restoration and degradation earn nothing under it. The next FRL is unfunded and depends on the NFI (A7).
3. **Mangrove soil carbon is the missing pool.** Excluded in both FRLs, accepted by assessors only for lack of data, and flagged twice as a source of underestimation (A4, A5). Cambodia's own SOC map ranks mangrove soil highest (A21). Peer FRLs (Viet Nam, Philippines, Kenya, Myanmar) also leave it out; Indonesia counts it only as a conversion (excavation) emission; Madagascar and Mexico include SOC (D1-D7).
4. **Standards require what the FRL omits.** VM0048 requires wetland soil carbon unless de minimis (C6); VM0033 includes the soil pool (C8); ART TREES 3.0 makes peat soil a primary pool and mineral soil secondary (C5). A project-level mangrove claim therefore carries more carbon accounting than the national baseline, which creates a nesting mismatch.
5. **REDD and VM0033 cannot be combined on the same hectare.** VM0033 excludes projects that qualify as REDD (C8). The choice for mangrove is avoided loss inside a REDD-type methodology (nested in the national forest accounting) or restoration under VM0033 (outside REDD+, and the only route for seagrass).
6. **Legal and institutional gaps.** The nesting design says all REDD+ projects must register and follow a Prakas (A14), but the sub-decree number, the Prakas number and their signature status are not confirmed at source (A15-A17). Mangrove outside protected areas is managed by the Fisheries Administration, not MoE (A12). The Carbon Crediting Secretariat instrument and the NRS revision are not public (A13).
7. **Article 6 positive list.** The Operations Manual authorises only activities that are "conditional" in the updated NDC; it already treats wetland restoration as a removal activity with 15-year renewable periods (A18). Seagrass is adaptation-only in NDC 3.0, so seagrass has no mitigation hook for authorisation.
8. **No overlap found with the biggest coastal-adjacent REDD+ project.** Southern Cardamom (VCS 1748) is registered as upland national park and wildlife sanctuary, 445,339 ha, no mangrove named (B1). Verra reinstated it in September 2024 with continuing conditions (B4). Marketing material claims mangrove under it (B3), so boundary files should be checked.
9. **No jurisdictional crediting route is evidenced.** No LEAF, ART or JNR engagement found (A20). No GCF payment found (A10). No Cambodian mangrove project found on the Verra pipeline, and the IBCI-FFI feasibility study is not public (B7, B8).
10. **Definition risk on stunted mangrove.** An assessor has called the "assume it will reach 5 m" approach cherry-picking in Madagascar (D5). Cambodia's rear mangrove and low coastal stands carry the same risk if mangrove is marketed as REDD+.
11. **Community rights.** Project-level REDD+ in Cambodia has drawn rights complaints and a Verra suspension (B4). A coastal scheme overlapping community fisheries needs the tenure basis (B9) settled first.
