# Note 1: The Seed Fund and where blue carbon fits in Cambodia's Country Package

## 1. Bottom line

Cambodia's Country Package under the Seed Fund for Country Packages for Forests, Nature and Climate (the Seed Fund), hosted by Conservation International (CI), is in its Design phase, and no Seed Fund page mentions blue carbon (here: mangroves, seagrass and tidal marsh). Whether coastal ecosystems are in the package is open: it is the government's call, led by the Ministry of Environment (MoE) if a consultant listing is right (U). Three entry points support a component: mangroves are forest under the 2002 Forestry Law (as CI Cambodia's report records), the third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0) names "blue carbon habitats" in a mitigation measure, and two global studies count mangrove and seagrass soils as irrecoverable carbon, mangroves on firmer evidence. That last point bears on one of four Phase 2 grant areas, but the Fund decides what qualifies, and national forest carbon accounting does not yet count mangrove soil.

## 2. The Seed Fund

All facts below were read on the Fund's pages and CI's launch release on 20 November 2026 (V).

- **Host and funders.** Hosted by CI; funded by the Government of France, the Rob Walton Foundation, the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation and CI ([About](https://seedfundfornature.org/about-the-seed-fund)). Launched at COP28 in December 2023 with EUR 115 million ([launch release](https://www.conservation.org/press/launch-of-the-seed-fund-for-country-packages-for-forests-nature-and-climate)).
- **Scale.** At launch, "up to € 8M" per Country Package, depending on carbon and biodiversity potential and the package's maturity. Current pages state no grant size.
- **Phase 1, design, 2026-2027.** Concept development; technical design; needs assessments; governance and institutional set-up; stakeholder engagement; vision and communication; costing and donor mapping ([Our work](https://seedfundfornature.org/our-work)).
- **Phase 2, implementation, 2027-2031.** Four grant areas: institutional capacity-building; "vital reserves of high biodiversity and irrecoverable carbon"; scientific research for conservation decisions; long-term financing and investor confidence. Irrecoverable carbon is one of four, not the headline, and no page defines it.
- **Routes and eligibility.** Open calls or "direct invitations for specialised work"; the only open call is for Huánuco, Peru; none for Cambodia. Civil society, public and research organisations may apply ([Grants](https://seedfundfornature.org/grants)).
- **Safeguards.** Applicants "must comply with Seed Fund safeguards, including requirements on stakeholder engagement, gender inclusion and grievance mechanisms".
- **Ecosystems named.** Mangroves, peatlands, old-growth forests and marshes (About). Seagrass is named nowhere.

## 3. Cambodia's package

Cambodia's page says only: "The Country Package is currently in the Design phase" ([Cambodia](https://seedfundfornature.org/countries/cambodia), V). The Forest and Climate Leaders' Partnership (FCLP) 2025 annex lists Cambodia among countries that "have expressed interest" ([FCLP annex](https://forestclimateleaders.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FCLP-2-Action-Areas-and-Progress-2025-EN.pdf), PDF p. 6, V); it is not among the seven launched packages. A June 2026 consultant listing for the "Country Package for Forests, Climate and Nature" in MoE's Department of Climate Change (DCC) suggests DCC holds the package (U: search snippet only).

The FCLP calls packages "Country-Led", with inter-ministerial coordination "to ensure full government ownership" (annex, PDF p. 5, V). CI's role is to support MoE: DCC, which coordinates the NDC and the greenhouse gas inventory (CI Cambodia 2026 report, p. 12), and the Department of Biodiversity, which plans a Blue Carbon Roadmap (p. 20).

Jurisdiction is split. CI's report places mangrove afforestation and reforestation projects outside protected areas under the General Directorate of Fisheries (GDF) of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF), and mangrove projects inside protected areas under MoE (p. 13). The report describes GDF as what the Fisheries Administration is "currently" called (p. 10). A component needs both ministries.

## 4. Entry points for blue carbon

REDD+ means reducing emissions from deforestation and forest degradation, plus conservation, sustainable forest management and enhancement of forest carbon stocks.

| Entry point | Type and lead | What the source says | Source | V/U |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mangroves are forest | Legal; MoE for REDD+ | "As mangroves are classified as a type of forest under the 2002 Forestry Law, they fall under the jurisdiction of RTF" (the REDD+ Taskforce); REDD+ later moved to MoE. The second forest reference level (2021) counts above- and below-ground biomass; soil is "excluded ... due to the lack of reliable data" | CI report, p. 12; [second Forest Reference Level (FRL)](https://redd.unfccc.int/files/cambodia_2nd_frl_modification_submission_20213005.pdf), PDF p. 16 | V (report, FRL); U (law text not read) |
| Irrecoverable carbon | Funding fit; the Fund decides | Global figures; the papers' methods and densities differ. Goldstein et al. (30-year window): mangroves hold 335 t C/ha irrecoverable, over 70 per cent in soil, second in their ranking only to tropical peat; whole-ecosystem recovery takes 153 years (mangroves) and 93 (seagrass). Noon et al. (to 2050): irrecoverable carbon of 3.15 Gt C in mangroves (3.01 in soil) and 1.53 Gt C in seagrass, all soil, from modelled extent | Goldstein et al. 2020, *Nature Climate Change* 10: 287-295, p. 290 and Table 2 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-020-0738-8)); Noon et al. 2022, *Nature Sustainability* 5: 37-46, Methods p. 43, Supplementary Table S12 ([CC BY 4.0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-021-00803-6)) | V (both full text) |
| NDC 3.0 measure 40 | Mitigation; MoE with MAFF | Assisted natural regeneration covering "sustainable management of forest and other resources including blue carbon habitats"; 4,080 ha of mangrove | [NDC 3.0](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-08/Cambodia-NDC%203.0_0.pdf), p. 26; CI report, p. 22 | V |
| NDC 3.0 measures 49 and 50 | Spatial planning and protected areas; MoE | Marine spatial planning to conserve and restore mangroves, seagrasses, coral reefs and oysters; more coastal and marine protected areas and other effective area-based conservation measures (OECMs) | NDC 3.0, p. 41 | V |
| NDC 3.0 adaptation measure 8 | Adaptation; MAFF | Restore 6,120 ha of mangrove and 4,167 ha of seagrass, each stated as 30 per cent of a baseline. The seagrass baseline printed in the table does not match 4,167 ha as 30 per cent | NDC 3.0, pp. 95-96; CI report, p. 22 | V (mangrove); U (seagrass figure) |
| NBSAP 2026-2030 Targets 3 and 11 | Area-based and nature's contributions; Department of Biodiversity | Target 3: "at least 10% of marine and coastal areas" conserved by protected areas and OECMs by 2030. Target 11 cites "coastal mangrove and seagrass restoration linked to livelihoods and emerging blue carbon opportunities" | National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (NBSAP), executive summary, PDF pp. 17, 24 ([Climate Policy Radar copy](https://cdn.climatepolicyradar.org/navigator/KHM/2026/cambodia-national-biodiversity-strategy-and-action-plan-nbsap-2026_f0f3eafadb8f7984a264c71b2348d8dd.pdf)) | V (official MoE copy not located) |
| Koh Kapik Ramsar Site | Area-based; MoE | 14,029 ha in Koh Kong, overlapping Peam Krasop Wildlife Sanctuary; mangrove extent not recorded | CI report, p. 3, Table 2 | V |
| Tropical Forest Forever Facility (TFFF) readiness | Finance readiness | Package support will help countries "assess their potential eligibility for TFFF and identify technical and institutional gaps" | [Seed Fund, 10 Nov 2026](https://seedfundfornature.org/insights/seed-fund-commits-1-million-to-tropical-forests-forever-facility) | V; U whether TFFF counts mangroves |

Only measure 40 is a mitigation commitment naming blue carbon habitats. The others support a component on planning, protected area and adaptation grounds: enough for a forests, nature and climate package, not for a credit case. Note 2 ([two tracks](02-blue-carbon-in-redd-plus-two-tracks.md)) covers how mangrove soil could enter REDD+ accounting.

On irrecoverable carbon, the Fund gives no definition, and its grant area joins two tests: "high biodiversity and irrecoverable carbon". Both papers stand in with the same three tests: carbon that local action can influence, that is vulnerable to conversion, and that could not be recovered within 30 years (Goldstein p. 289), or "by 2050 if lost today" (Noon pp. 37-38). Mangrove soil meets all three: conversion to shrimp ponds loses about 80 per cent of soil carbon in the top metre (Goldstein p. 289). Both papers count seagrass soil in their estimates, but the vulnerability test is weakly evidenced: Noon et al. call its loss on conversion "uncertain", and model its extent and stock. Neither paper gives a Cambodian coastal figure; Noon et al.'s Cambodian hotspot is the forest around Tonle Sap. The defensible line: coastal sites combining mangrove or seagrass soil carbon with high biodiversity are candidates, for the Fund to judge; mangroves are the stronger case.
