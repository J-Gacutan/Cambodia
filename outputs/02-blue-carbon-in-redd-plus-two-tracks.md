# Note 2 — Blue carbon in Cambodia's REDD+: two tracks

## 1. Bottom line

Cambodia should bring blue carbon into REDD+ on two tracks. Track A deepens mangroves inside REDD+: a mangrove stratum, plus soil carbon, degradation and enhancement in the next Forest Reference Level (FRL), with the greenhouse gas (GHG) inventory reporting mangrove soil on the same basis. Track B gives seagrass and other non-forest coastal wetlands their own accounting under the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) Wetlands Supplement, sharing REDD+ institutions, safeguards, registry and benefit sharing, but not REDD+ accounting or results-based payments. Conservation International (CI) Cambodia's June 2026 report already holds most of Track B; this note adds a firm choice, a sequence and nesting rules, and differs on two points: mangrove soil belongs in the next FRL, and seagrass's Article 6 ineligibility is a national rule the Ministry of Environment (MoE) can change.

**Status:** internal working note for Jordan Gacutan, not for forwarding. Evidence as of 20 November 2026, desk-based, except the UNFCCC reference-level list, the Article 6.2 initial report, Verra project 1748 and the positive-list reading, which were checked on 22 November 2026. Gates run: verify pass, hostile red team, director gate (see the end of the note).

REDD+ means reducing emissions from deforestation and forest degradation, plus conservation, sustainable management of forests and enhancement of forest carbon stocks. "CI report" means CI Cambodia's *Policy and finance analysis and stakeholder mapping* (June 2026), cited by printed page.

## 3. The forest-only rule

Decision 1/CP.16, para 70, "encourages developing country Parties to contribute to mitigation actions in the forest sector" through five activities: reducing emissions from deforestation; reducing emissions from forest degradation; conservation of forest carbon stocks; sustainable management of forests; and enhancement of forest carbon stocks (FCCC/CP/2010/7/Add.1, p. 12). Para 71 ties the strategy, FRL, monitoring and safeguards systems to "the activities referred to in paragraph 70". Under the Warsaw Framework, to "obtain and receive results-based finance", actions "should be fully measured, reported and verified" (decision 9/CP.19, para 3), and results must be "consistent over time and with the established" FRL (decision 14/CP.19, para 3). Three consequences follow.

- **Seagrass and tidal flats cannot be REDD+ activities** — they fail the forest definition. The Wetlands Supplement classes seagrass and tidal marsh as Wetlands, and calls it "good practice to report mangroves in the appropriate national land-use category according to the national forest definition" (IPCC 2013, Ch. 4, Table 4.1 note 2).
- **A wider mandate gives oversight, not results** — revised RTF and RTS terms of reference let them review, register and safeguard seagrass projects, but cannot create REDD+ results-based payments, since no seagrass outcome is measured against an FRL.
- **The definition decides the track, hectare by hectare** — mangrove meeting the thresholds is Forest Land (Track A). Mangrove failing them is Wetlands (Track B). Decision 12/CP.17 allows an explained difference between FRL and inventory definitions (annex, item (d)), but one definition throughout is safer for nesting.

## 4. Track A — mangroves inside REDD+

Under the current FRL, mangrove restoration and avoided degradation earn nothing. Cambodia's own plan is to add degradation, enhancement and soil data to the next FRL (second FRL, PDF p. 41); Track A writes mangroves into it.

| Change | Milestone | Responsible body |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A mangrove stratum in the National Forest Monitoring System (NFMS), out of "Other forest" | Next land-cover release | MoE (NFMS), with GDF data |
| 2. Mangrove emission factors including soil (Wetlands Supplement Ch. 4): Tier 1 now, Tier 2 later | Next BTR | MoE measurement, reporting and verification (MRV) team |
| 3. Mangrove soil in the next FRL, conditional on GHG inventory consistency, plus degradation and enhancement | Next FRL | MoE (FRL team, RTS); assessed under 13/CP.19 |
| 4. Mangrove policies and measures in the revised NRS (2027 onward) | NRS revision | MoE (RTF, RTS), with MAFF |
| 5. Nesting rules for mangrove projects, covering existing VCS projects and both MoE and GDF jurisdictions | Before new mangrove projects register | RTS and the Carbon Crediting Secretariat (CCS), with GDF |
| 6. The safeguards information system (SIS) extended to coastal tenure, community fisheries (CFi), community protected areas (CPA) and gender | Next safeguards summary | MoE (RTS Safeguard Team), with MAFF |

**Inventory and FRL on the same basis.** Decision 12/CP.17 requires FRLs to be established "maintaining consistency with anthropogenic forest-related greenhouse gas emissions by sources and removals by sinks as contained in each country's greenhouse gas inventories" (para 8), and allows a "step-wise approach" adding "where appropriate, additional pools" (para 10). The 13/CP.19 assessment tests "the extent to which" the FRL keeps that consistency (annex, para 2(a)). That is a test, not a rule on order: both can add mangrove soil together, or a difference can be explained. This note recommends the inventory first, through the next BTR, because BTR1 has no mangrove soil term and the next FRL has no date.

**What the assessors said.** In 2017 they accepted the soil exclusion "because of the lack of spatial data on the extent of organic soils", noting that omitting pools "could result in the underestimation of historical emissions, thereby reducing the risk of overestimating future emission reductions" (FCCC/TAR/2017/KHM, para 25). In 2021 Cambodia explained that "a full NFI has not yet been conducted" and planned to add all five pools "subject to funding of a full NFI" (FCCC/TAR/2021/KHM, paras 29-30). The assessors said the exclusion "could result in an underestimation of emissions", pointed to IPCC default methods and factors, and called it "an area for future technical improvement" (para 30). Neither report mentions mangrove soil. Defaults need not wait for the national forest inventory (NFI).

**The case is consistency, not national significance.** Mangrove deforestation in the second FRL totals about 1,110 ha over 2011-2018, roughly 139 ha a year (Table 10). Even if all 386 tC/ha in the top metre were emitted (Wetlands Supplement, Table 4.11), that is about 196,000 tCO2 a year, 0.3% of the 60.3 MtCO2e FRL; at Indonesia's factor, 0.02%. Mangrove soil belongs in the FRL because nested mangrove projects must count it (below), and one national reference for those hectares avoids double counting. Results-based payment eligibility is secondary: no Green Climate Fund payment, and no Cambodian engagement with the Architecture for REDD+ Transactions (ART), LEAF Coalition or Verra's jurisdictional framework, was found.

**The interim limit.** At Tier 1, "soil CO2 emissions and removals are zero (EF=0) for forest management practices in mangroves"; soil emissions from forest clearing are left to higher tiers (Ch. 4, p. 4.17). Tier 1 counts soil loss only where a site is excavated for ponds or drained, so clearance that leaves soil in place scores zero until Cambodia has Tier 2 factors. Enhancement does better: Tier 1 gives mangrove rewetting and revegetation a soil removal factor of 1.62 tC/ha a year (Table 4.12), so restoration on former ponds can count soil now.

For conversion, this note recommends the structure of Indonesia's assessed FRL, which counts "only peat and mangrove soil carbon" as an emission on the area converted each year, by destination (PDF p. 18). It applies 90.06 tCO2e/ha for fishponds, a measured national value (Arifanti et al. 2019), and 28.97 tCO2e/ha for cultivated land, an IPCC default (Table 11, PDF p. 32): 554,210 tCO2e a year from 9,017 ha a year (Tables 34 and 19; components sum to 9,018). The fishpond factor is far below the Tier 1 extraction ceiling of about 1,400 tCO2/ha. Cambodia should report both as a range until project measurements supply a national factor.

**Definition risk.** Rear mangrove and low stands may not reach 5 m. Madagascar acknowledged that its mangroves "do not naturally reach a height of 5 m", and the assessors said its approach "may be interpreted as the country selectively choosing (sometimes referred to as 'cherry-picking')" (FCCC/TAR/2018/MDG, para 41, PDF p. 11). The stratum should be mapped against height and crown-cover evidence; stands that fail go to Track B.

**Three nesting rules the design lacks.**

- **Soil mismatch** — Verra VM0048 requires REDD projects in wetlands to account for soil "unless deemed de minimis" (v1.0, p. 9). ART TREES 3.0 makes mineral soil a secondary pool, excludable if conservative or under its 3% and 10% thresholds (PDF p. 52). Rule: project soil reductions sit outside the REDD+ results allocation until soil enters the FRL, and any Article 6 authorisation of them needs a corresponding adjustment regardless (2/CMA.3 annex, paras 13-14). Once the inventory reports mangrove soil, the registry must also flag them against the national total. When soil enters the FRL, project baselines reset to it from a stated vintage.
- **Methodology split** — VM0033 v2.1 does not apply where "Project activities qualify as IFM or REDD" (PDF p. 12). Standing-mangrove conservation goes through a REDD route nested in the FRL; restoration on former ponds through VM0033. Both need an allocation.
- **Two ministries** — the Action and Investment Plan requires that "all REDD+ projects must register formally" (2020, PDF p. 24-25). The rule should extend to GDF-led mangrove projects outside protected areas.

## 5. Track B — seagrass and other non-forest coastal wetlands

Track B covers seagrass, tidal marsh and mangrove that fails the forest thresholds. Cambodia has about 13,890 ha of seagrass (CI report, p. V). The first Cambodian seagrass estimate is 122 ± 52 tC/ha in the top metre at Chrouy Pros Bay, 56% to 95% of it allochthonous, that is, produced elsewhere (Krause et al. 2025, *Science of the Total Environment* 993; abstract read).

- **Coastal wetland inventory and reference level** — under Wetlands Supplement Chapter 4, first for planning and benchmarking, as the CI report proposes (p. 36). Tier 1 covers seagrass revegetation (0.43 tC/ha a year) and extraction, not loss from turbidity, so early accounts will mostly track area. Precedent is narrow: Australia "includes seagrass under the wetlands land use category", but only for 416 ha dredged in 1990-2016 (National Inventory Report 2024, Vol. 2, p. 315), and the United States says "Seagrasses are not currently included" (US Environmental Protection Agency inventory 1990-2022, p. 6-109). Cambodia would be early, not late.
- **Terms of reference revision** — the CI report recommends expanding the RTF, RTS and Technical Teams' roles "to include oversight and coordination of blue carbon ecosystems, particularly seagrass" (p. 46). This note adopts it for review, registration, safeguards, benefit sharing and monitoring; it creates no REDD+ activity, FRL entry or results-based payments.
- **Seagrass in a future Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) mitigation scope** — the CI report recommends this (p. 22, p. 47); NDC 3.0 has it only in adaptation (measure 8). This note adds that, once the manual's list is re-pointed at the current NDC, listing it among conditional measures also puts it on the Article 6 positive list.
- **Crediting routes** — the voluntary carbon market through VM0033 v2.1, which covers seagrass restoration only and does not credit allochthonous carbon. Credits depend on new soil accumulation net of its allochthonous share; the high share in the Chrouy Pros stock suggests, but does not prove, a large deduction. A Verra seagrass conservation route (VMD0051) is unconfirmed.
- **System of Environmental-Economic Accounting (SEEA) Ecosystem Accounting** — extent, condition and service accounts (coastal protection, fisheries nurseries) benchmark non-carbon finance where carbon volumes are small (UN et al. 2021).

**Article 6 for seagrass is a national rule MoE can change.** The Operations Manual (Council of Ministers letter 1380, 14 December 2023) limits authorisation to a "positive list" of "mitigation activities" (PDF p. 21) that "encompasses all activities designated as 'conditional' in the RGC's updated NDC" (PDF p. 22); its footnote 19 already names "wetland restoration" as a nature-based removal. Decision 2/CMA.3 has no positive list and never uses "conditional". It counts "mitigation co-benefits resulting from adaptation actions" as mitigation outcomes (annex, para 1(b)) and allows transfers from removals "not covered by its NDC", with a corresponding adjustment (annex, para 14). The barrier to authorisation is therefore Cambodia's positive list, not the inventory, SIS or FRL gaps as such. 2/CMA.3 still sets integrity conditions, including that reversals of removals are "addressed in full" (annex, paras 18(h)(iii) and 22), and these would bind any seagrass outcome once the list is widened.

MoE can change it in two ways: revise the positive list, or put seagrass in the next NDC's conditional mitigation measures and re-point the list at that NDC. The manual scheduled its own revision "in 2025" and applies its criteria "until December 2025" (PDF p. 18). The file at the MoE URL, fetched 22 November 2026, is still the text of 17 January 2024, and the list still points to the 2020 NDC update (fn 17). NDC 3.0 (August 2025) labels measures U, C or U/C: seagrass restoration is in adaptation measure 8 (C), and the 4,080 ha mangrove target is in mitigation measure 40 (U/C) (PDF p. 44, p. 97, pp. 105-106). A C-labelled adaptation measure does not fall within the list. Criterion 1 admits only projects on a "positive list" of "mitigation activities", and that list "encompasses all activities designated as 'conditional'" in the updated NDC the manual cites, the 31 December 2020 update (PDF p. 21-22, fn 17). The Article 6.2 initial report (version 1.0, dated 14 February 2025; CARP date 30 April 2025) restates the same rule, "activities in the updated NDC that have been designated as conditional upon international support", and cites the 2020 update (PDF p. 3). It does not mention NDC 3.0 or adaptation measures. Re-pointing the footnote at NDC 3.0 would still leave measure 8 outside a list of mitigation activities, so MoE must widen the list, or place seagrass in conditional mitigation, before authorisation. Credible authorisation still needs the inventory.

## 6. Shared infrastructure

Track B uses REDD+ machinery rather than its own. They are named here, not resolved.

- **Registry** — not yet built; UNFCCC or issuing-mechanism registries are used meanwhile (Operations Manual, PDF p. 19-23), and the RTS and CCS overlap on it (CI report, p. 17, p. 46).
- **Benefit sharing, grievance, free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) and carbon rights** — none yet specific to carbon credits or legally settled (CI report, p. VI).
- **Safeguards** — one SIS, extended to coastal tenure and fisheries rights (CI report, p. 35), plus gender.

## 7. Sequencing

The recommended order is the inventory before the FRL, or both together. Everything else can start now.

On the first difference, the report does not say how a mangrove coastal reference level would relate to the FRL; this note settles it. Mangroves are Forest Land, so their soil belongs with their biomass, and two reference levels for the same hectares would leave nested projects unsure which applies. A planning benchmark, as the report intends at first, remains useful. On the second, the report ties eligibility to the NDC and national systems; the note narrows this to the instrument that gates authorisation, and adds that 2/CMA.3 requires neither a positive list nor NDC coverage.

## 9. Options set aside

- **(a) Mangrove soil in a separate coastal reference level** — one reading of the report's coastal reference levels "starting with mangroves" (p. 36), with coastal accounting outside the FRL (p. 34). It can be built without waiting for an FRL submission and suits planning. Set aside as the official home for mangrove soil because it splits one land category's pools across two benchmarks; kept as a planning benchmark.
- **(b) A standalone blue carbon strategy instead of NRS integration** — the report's alternative, a "parallel mechanism (standalone national blue carbon strategy)" focused on seagrass (pp. 22, 47). It would give coastal ecosystems clear ownership. Set aside because it would duplicate the safeguards, registry and benefit sharing that Track B shares, the risk of "separate, overlapping systems" the report raises for safeguards (p. 35).
- **(c) The status quo** — no cost now, but restoration keeps earning nothing nationally, soil accounting stays out of step, and seagrass relies on voluntary standards.

## 10. What the Seed Fund could finance

Phase 1 (2026-2027) funds design across seven work areas; Phase 2 (2027-2031) funds implementation. These items are inference, as no Cambodia template has been seen; Note 1 ([01-seed-fund-country-package-blue-carbon.md](01-seed-fund-country-package-blue-carbon.md)) covers the programme.

- **Track A** — technical design of stratum mapping and soil sampling; draft nesting rules for MoE and GDF projects; a costed NFI and next-FRL plan, the unfunded dependency.
- **Track B** — seagrass inventory design and a survey-based baseline map (Note 3b's restricted seagrass analogue only flags known meadows and loss: [03b-restoration-potential-screen-method.md](03b-restoration-potential-screen-method.md)); drafting support for the terms of reference revision; an options paper on the positive list; a provincial SEEA account pilot.
- **Shared** — SIS coastal extension, FPIC and gender guidance, and scoping of carbon benefit-sharing and grievance mechanisms.
