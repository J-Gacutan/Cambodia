## 3.0 Central Argument

The argument has three claims. Each comes with a test that would defeat it.

### Claim 1: blue carbon belongs in Cambodia's Country Package

Two entry points need no new legal classification:
- Mangroves are already forest under the 2002 Forestry Law and inside REDD+ institutions.
- Mangrove and seagrass soils hold large stores of irrecoverable carbon, which matches one of the Seed Fund's four Phase 2 grant areas.

**Defeated if:**
- CI Cambodia confirms the package scope already excludes coastal ecosystems by government decision; or
- the irrecoverable-carbon literature, read at source, does not cover Cambodia's mangrove or seagrass types. In that case Note 1 relies on the forest-law and NDC entry points alone.

### Claim 2: two tracks give a firm, sequenced route the report leaves open

**Track A, mangroves inside REDD+.** Mangroves get a distinct NFMS stratum. Mangrove soil carbon goes into the next Forest Reference Level (FRL), not into a separate coastal reference level, so that Cambodia keeps one consistent national forest account and mangroves are not counted twice. Results-based payment eligibility is preserved, but it is a secondary reason, since no REDD+ results-based payment or jurisdictional buyer (GCF, ART/LEAF, JNR) is currently in view for Cambodia (research file A10, A20). This is the first point where Note 2 departs from the report's p. 36 framing.

The soil change is conditional. It can enter the FRL only once the GHG inventory reports mangrove soil consistently, and BTR1 omits wetlands. The next FRL is also unfunded and depends on a national forest inventory (research A7).

The next FRL must add degradation and enhancement as well as the new stratum and soil, which matches Cambodia's own stated plan (research A7). Otherwise restoration still earns nothing.

Interim limit: Wetlands Supplement Tier 1 sets the mangrove soil factor to zero for forest clearance and counts soil only on excavation (research C4). Until Tier 2 factors exist, Note 2 recommends Indonesia's assessed form, which counts mangrove soil as a conversion emission (research D1), with project-level measurements alongside.

Definition risk: stunted rear mangrove may fail the 5 m height threshold. UNFCCC assessors called Madagascar's "assume it reaches 5 m" approach potential cherry-picking (research D5).

**Track B, seagrass and other non-forest coastal wetlands on their own track.** Accounting follows the IPCC Wetlands Supplement, with a coastal reference level used first for planning.

- **What Track B adopts from the report.** It endorses the p. 45 recommendation to revise the RTF and RTS terms of reference, so that seagrass gains an institutional home, safeguards, the registry and benefit sharing.
- **What a mandate change cannot do.** It gives oversight, not REDD+ results-based payments. Under decision 1/CP.16, para 70, REDD+ activities cover forest only.
- **Article 6 for seagrass is a national rule MoE can change.** The Operations Manual, approved 14 December 2023, limits its positive list to activities designated "conditional" in the NDC (research file, V). The manual's footnote 19 already names wetland restoration as a nature-based removal. Seagrass is ineligible today because of Cambodia's own positive list, not because of decision 2/CMA.3. MoE can open it either by revising the manual or by putting seagrass in the next NDC's conditional mitigation measures. This refines the report's pp. 44–45 statement rather than contradicting it. The report's p. 17 point, that a ToR revision is needed for seagrass to "be eligible", is institutional and is taken up under Track B.

**Track A diagnosis (research file, V).** Cambodia's second FRL (2021, reference period 2011–2018) has these features:
- It covers deforestation only.
- It counts above- and below-ground biomass only.
- It folds mangroves into "Other forest", using a single factor of 151.43 tCO2e/ha.

So mangrove degradation and restoration earn nothing under it.

The FRL also omits soil carbon. UNFCCC assessors accepted this in 2017 and in 2021 noted that it could underestimate emissions. Meanwhile Verra VM0048 requires wetland soil carbon unless it is de minimis, and ART TREES 3.0 makes peat soil a primary pool and mineral soil a secondary pool that may be excluded under its 3%/10% rule. That leaves a project-versus-national gap, and the nesting design has no rule for it.

**Defeated if:**
- a later FRL, submitted after 2021, already adds a mangrove stratum or soil carbon. Track A would then reduce to maintenance. Path B checks the UNFCCC REDD+ submissions page;
- the Operations Manual has since been revised to drop the NDC-conditional positive list. Path B notes the planned 2025 revision as unconfirmed (U).

### Claim 3: projects are best screened by stage and by map

Projects should pass a staged screen specific to Cambodia, in which non-carbon outcomes and legal gates carry thresholds of their own. The first pre-feasibility filter is a national restoration-potential map built from mangrove change and aquaculture and urban expansion over time.

**Defeated if:**
- the open spatial products cannot separate aquaculture from other mangrove loss at the coastal scale of Cambodia's four provinces. Note 3b then specifies a reduced screen and says so.

#### Note 2 — Blue carbon in Cambodia's REDD+: two tracks

File: `outputs/02-blue-carbon-in-redd-plus-two-tracks.md`

- **Today's position.**
  - Mangroves are forest under the 2002 Forestry Law.
  - The FRL includes mangroves only where they meet the forest definition. It has no distinct mangrove category and no soil organic carbon (CI report, p. 35; research file).
  - The National REDD+ Strategy 2017–2026 covers mangroves only.
  - The RTF and RTS mandates cover mangroves.
  - BTR1 omits wetlands.
  - Coastal projects: two REDD+ projects and one ARR mangrove project (CI report, Table 6).
  - Jurisdiction is split between MoE and GDF/FiA.
- **The forest-only rule.** What decision 1/CP.16 para 70 and the Warsaw Framework cover, with the decision text quoted, and what that means for seagrass and tidal flats.
- **Track A changes.** Each change is tied to a milestone and a responsible body:
  1. a distinct mangrove stratum in the NFMS;
  2. mangrove emission factors including soil, from Wetlands Supplement Chapter 4;
  3. soil carbon in the next FRL, **conditional on GHG inventory consistency** (12/CP.17 annex; technical assessment under 13/CP.19);
  4. mangrove policies and measures in the revised National REDD+ Strategy (2027 onward);
  5. nesting rules for mangrove projects, covering existing VCS projects and both MoE and GDF jurisdictions;
  6. the SIS extended to coastal tenure, community fisheries (CFi), community protected areas (CPA) and gender.
- **Track B changes.**
  - A national coastal wetland inventory and reference level under the Wetlands Supplement, first for planning and benchmarking. Cite a precedent country that reports seagrass or coastal wetlands in its inventory, from research.
  - The RTF and RTS ToR revision the report recommends, with its scope and limits stated.
  - Seagrass in a future NDC mitigation scope.
  - Crediting routes: the voluntary carbon market via VM0033 (current version), and Article 6 once MoE opens it, through a national rule change (the Operations Manual's positive list, or seagrass in the next NDC's conditional mitigation measures).
  - A link to SEEA Ecosystem Accounting, for benchmarking non-carbon values.
- **Shared infrastructure.** Registry, benefit-sharing mechanism, grievance mechanism, FPIC, and carbon rights, to be named as cross-cutting items, not resolved.
- **Sequencing** against these milestones:
  - National REDD+ Strategy revision, end 2026;
  - next BTR;
  - next FRL;
  - NDC update cycle;
  - Seed Fund Phase 1 and Phase 2.
  Dependencies are drawn explicitly. In particular, inventory consistency comes before soil carbon enters the FRL.
- **"Where this builds on and where it differs from the CI Cambodia report"** table. Rows quote the report's position by page: pp. VI, 17, 35, 36, 44–46. Columns: agree / adds / differs.
- **Options set aside.** These are the real alternatives, described accurately and not as strawmen:
  - (a) mangrove soil in a separate coastal reference level, the report's p. 36 framing;
  - (b) a standalone blue carbon strategy with no link to the National REDD+ Strategy, which the report itself weighs at p. 45;
  - (c) the status quo.
- **What the Seed Fund could finance**, item by item for each track.

#### Note 3 — Blue carbon project selection and feasibility framework

File: `outputs/03-blue-carbon-project-selection-feasibility-framework.md`

- **Stage-gates.** Four stages, aligned with the Article 6 Operations Manual's three-phase project cycle:
  1. idea;
  2. pre-feasibility;
  3. feasibility;
  4. design and authorisation.

  For each stage, give:
  - its purpose and the evidence required;
  - go / caution / stop criteria;
  - the responsible Cambodian body;
  - typical cost and time, where sources allow.
- **Criteria, in six groups.**
  - **Carbon.**
    - Method eligibility: VM0033 excludes projects that qualify as REDD, so standing-mangrove conservation goes through a REDD route.
    - Stocks.
    - Baseline.
    - Additionality.
    - Permanence.
    - Leakage.
  - **Ecological and site suitability.**
    - Hydrology.
    - Restoration potential, taken from Note 3b's screen classes.
    - Sea-level rise.
  - **Legal and authorisation.**
    - Letter of No Objection and Letter of Authorisation, via the CCS (or the RTS for forest projects).
    - Marine National Park permits from GDNPA and GDLC.
    - Marine Fisheries Management Area permits from FiA/GDF, under the 2025 Law on Fisheries.
    - Review by the national and provincial coastal committees (NCCMD and PCCMD).
    - Tenure through MLMUPC.
    - Ramsar site status.
  - **Social and safeguards.**
    - CFi and CPA rights.
    - FPIC.
    - Gender inclusion.
    - Benefit sharing.
    - Grievance.
  - **Non-carbon value.**
    - Coastal protection.
    - Fisheries.
    - Livelihoods.
    - Biodiversity.
    - Adaptation finance.
    - PES.
    - Biodiversity credits.
  - **Finance and delivery.**
    - Scale.
    - Cost per hectare.
    - Credit or non-credit revenue.
    - MRV capacity.
- **Weighting of non-carbon outcomes.** They are criteria with thresholds, not co-benefits. A project may pass on a non-market route with no carbon credit.
- **Selection rubric.** Weights are transparent and sensitivity-tested on **four stylised archetypes**, not on real sites:
  - (i) restoring mangrove on abandoned shrimp ponds;
  - (ii) CFi-managed mangrove conservation;
  - (iii) seagrass protection inside a Marine Fisheries Management Area;
  - (iv) mixed mangrove and seagrass inside a Marine National Park.
  Show how rankings shift under carbon-heavy weights and under non-carbon-heavy weights.
- **Restoration-potential screen.** A one-page summary of Note 3b, with a link to it.
- **Data gaps and desk stand-ins.**
